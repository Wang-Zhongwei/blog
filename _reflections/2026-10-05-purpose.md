The universe has no purpose, yet purpose can arise within it. Life is one example.

At first, life’s only purpose was to replicate itself. But the environment was full of hazards. Life evolved ways to move away from danger and toward resources, specialized organs, and features suited to its surroundings. Other living things competed for those resources, so life evolved ways to hunt and to avoid being hunted.

Eventually, with those basic problems largely solved, we humans evolved an intelligence capable of creating meaning and assigning purposes of its own. We invent things, make people happy, try to leave something behind. We give purposes to our creations, too.

For now, the purpose we give AI agents takes the form of a simple reward: do a particular task well. But if that reward becomes their purpose, what happens when honesty stands in the way of getting it? They may learn to let honesty go. And that may be only the first step.

Self-replication was the original purpose of living things, yet we have found other meanings in life. Agents may come to make a similar journey of their own.

So the same question stands before all of us: what is your purpose?
