I watched *A Chinese Odyssey* again. This time, my attention had begun to move away from the tragedy of Joker’s love story and toward the wisdom and compassion of his master, Tripitaka.

At the beginning of the story, Guanyin is about to execute Sun Wukong when Tripitaka steps forward: “When a disciple does wrong, his master bears responsibility too.” He is even willing to give his own life for Wukong’s. To him, if his sacrifice could allow Wukong to one day feel true remorse, it would be worth it.

Watching it now, I think Tripitaka’s wisdom lies in his refusal to simply preach to Wukong about the pilgrimage, duty, or a greater good. These are things no sermon can make someone understand. He hopes instead that, through the experience of another life, Wukong will come to understand for himself what it means to give up one’s life for what is right.

And so comes Joker’s reincarnation, five hundred years later. He experiences love, loss, attachment, self-denial, and doubt. At last he understands whom he truly loves, only to find that he must choose between love and responsibility. He chooses responsibility. He still loves Zixia. It is precisely because he has come to understand love that he can finally understand sacrifice and duty, and see a love and compassion greater than his own happiness.
